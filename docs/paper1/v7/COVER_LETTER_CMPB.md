# Cover letter — Computer Methods and Programs in Biomedicine

Dear Editors,

Please consider the enclosed original research article, "Hemoglyph: an offline, guideline-traceable laboratory-panel interpretation engine with 33,183 rules, 99.2% AI-drafted, and four methods for verifying it against its sources, executable invariants and a frontier LLM", for publication in *Computer Methods and Programs in Biomedicine*.

**Why this submission is appropriate for CMPB.** The paper is about computing methodology and demonstrable software: how a clinical rule base that was drafted at scale with large language models can be treated as software and verified. It describes three reproducible methods—property-based executable invariants, a source-concordance protocol frozen before sampling, and a hash-frozen one-shot benchmark with a supervised baseline, an ablation and a blinded LLM comparison—and applies them to an openly released engine (AGPL-3.0) and rule registry (CC BY-SA 4.0) with released evaluation scripts, prompts, outputs and freeze manifests. This matches the journal's aims of formal computing methods, demonstrable software and the improvement of existing software.

**What is known.** Rule-based laboratory decision support is traceable but hand-maintained and rarely evaluated outside its home institution; supervised models and LLMs scale but cannot be audited clause by clause, and LLM benchmarks in medicine have been criticised for unstable metrics, leakage and post-hoc selection. AI-assisted drafting now makes very large rule bases cheap to produce, but no method has been described for verifying them, and the main public repository of executable guideline knowledge (AHRQ CDS Connect) went offline in 2025.

**What this study adds.** (i) An invariant suite that found a ranking defect which 353 adversarial vignettes had missed; (ii) a concordance protocol whose result is unfavourable to our own system—92 % of sampled rules could not be checked from their locators and at least 7.5 % were demonstrably discordant—leading to the methodological conclusion that provenance granularity, not rule count, limits auditability, together with a documented failure mode of human raters that any such audit must guard against; (iii) a frozen benchmark on more than 20,000 public encounters in which a failed quarantine round is reported, an untrained and fully traceable engine reaches recall@5 parity with an unnamed frontier LLM, and a prevalence baseline shows that the engine's contribution is ordering rather than coverage.

**Declarations.** The manuscript has not been published and is not under consideration elsewhere; a preprint may be deposited.
